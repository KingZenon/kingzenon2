Predicting road accident severity in Thailand

A study of crash severity (fatal, injury, property damage only) on Thailand's Ministry of Transport road network.

01 / THE PROBLEM

Detect the crashes that matter most.

Fatal crashes are about 11% of incidents in the audited data (19,437 of 170,022). Overall accuracy alone can hide missed fatal cases, so the evaluation reports fatal-class recall together with precision, macro-F1 and balanced accuracy.

01

Research question

Which model and imbalance strategy improves fatal-crash detection across three severity classes (fatal, injury, property damage only) while keeping false alarms useful?

02

Factors

Examine the time, location, road, vehicle, and cause fields in the audited files. Casualty counts define the target, so they are kept out of the features.

03

Generalisation

Train on 2019–2024, validate on 2025 and test once on 2026 to measure change over time. A comparison with another country's data is planned.

Related work

Mohamad et al. (2025), read in full, compared eight classifiers on 112,837 Thai motorcycle accidents (2015–2020) and found random forest best (accuracy 0.777) but fatal recall only 0.198. Champahom et al. (2024) report XGBoost as the strongest tree-based model on Thai highways, and Uttra et al. (2026) report XGBoost best (validation accuracy 83.8%) for three-class holiday-period crashes in Thailand. Phojaem et al. (2026) compare injury-severity models for highway-speed motorways. Gradient-boosted trees therefore look like the strongest candidates, and weak fatal-crash recall is the common problem. This project adds a multi-year, three-class benchmark on the Ministry of Transport network with temporal validation. Details beyond the first paper come from abstracts and are still being checked against the full texts. See the reference list below.

03 / METHOD

Reproducible analysis workflow

This is the analysis plan. Completed steps and changes are recorded in the progress log.

01

Prepare

Merge yearly files, harmonise names, dates and categories, document missing values, and derive a three-class severity target (fatal, injury, property damage only).

02

Compare

Start from a majority-class baseline, logistic regression, decision tree, random forest, XGBoost and LightGBM. Then compare class weights, SMOTE and tuned thresholds.

03

Validate

Validate on 2025 and keep 2026 as a held-out test used once at the end. Use cross-validation only within training years and report the exact split.

04

Interpret

Report fatal-class recall, precision, F1, PR-AUC, macro-F1, balanced accuracy, confusion matrices, and meaningful error patterns.

Potential hotspots can be explored after location coverage and data quality are checked. Any clustering method and evaluation will be documented with the results.
